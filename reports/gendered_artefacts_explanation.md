# Specific Artefacts Associated with Women, Children, and Families

Analysis of gender and family associations in archaeological features

---

## Summary

Only **2 features** were explicitly tagged as associated with **Women**. No features were explicitly tagged with "Children" or "Family". The women's association is based specifically on **perfume bottles** as the key gendered artefact type.

---

## Feature 6011 (Nellie's Glen) - Tagged: Women

**Feature Type:** Artefact scatter in possible cesspit/waste pit

**Specific Artefacts Present:**

1. **3 fragments of small manganese perfume/pharmacy bottle** ← PRIMARY GENDERING INDICATOR
2. 5 ceramic blue banded ware fragments (domestic tableware)

**Rationale for Women's Association:**

- **Perfume bottles**: Historically, perfume and cosmetic bottles are strongly associated with women's personal care practices
- **Small manganese glass bottles**: This glass type (purple-tinted from manganese dioxide) was commonly used for perfumes, cosmetics, and women's pharmaceutical products in the late 19th century
- **Cesspit/waste pit context**: Domestic waste disposal and household maintenance were traditionally within women's sphere of labour
- **Combined evidence**: Perfume + domestic ceramics + household waste management = women's presence

---

## Feature 8008 (Nellie's Glen) - Tagged: Women

**Feature Type:** Hearth with associated artefacts

**Specific Artefacts Present:**

1. **Base of thick-walled clear perfume bottle** (heat-affected/molten) ← PRIMARY GENDERING INDICATOR
2. Single collar rim (bottle component)
3. White ironstone saucer/plate base with floral sprig decoration

**Rationale for Women's Association:**

- **Perfume bottle**: Personal care item traditionally associated with women
- **Heat-affected glass**: Found near/in hearth, suggesting domestic activity context
- **Decorated ironstone tableware**: Quality domestic ceramics indicating household setting and care in domestic material culture
- **Combined evidence**: Perfume bottle + decorated tableware + domestic hearth = women's domestic presence

## Children and Families

**No features were explicitly tagged with "Children" or "Family" associations.**

**Search Results:**
- No toys, dolls, marbles, or other children's items documented
- No explicit references to children in feature descriptions
- No small-scale or miniature items interpreted as children's possessions

**Indirect Evidence of Family Presence:**

While not explicitly tagged, several features contain artefacts suggesting family or household settings rather than bachelor camps:

1. **Chamber pots** (multiple features with bone china chamber pots)
   - Suggest domestic privacy and sanitation concerns
   - More typical of family/household settings than transient camps

2. **Diverse domestic assemblages** (95+ features)
   - Complete range of tableware (teacups, saucers, plates, bowls)
   - Food preparation vessels
   - Serving ware and dining equipment
   - Suggests structured household meals rather than rough camp eating

3. **Decorated and quality ceramics**
   - Investment in aesthetic domestic goods
   - Care in household material culture
   - Indicates settled domestic life and household pride

## Critical Analysis

### Strengths of Evidence:

**Perfume bottles** are a relatively strong indicator:
- Clear gendered consumption pattern in 19th-century Australia
- Not typically associated with male miners' personal care
- Found in domestic contexts (hearth, waste pit) rather than work areas

### Limitations:

1. **Small sample size**: Only 2 features (0.7% of all features) explicitly gendered

2. **Interpretive framework**: Tagging reflects historical gender assumptions:
   - Perfume = women
   - Domestic work = women's sphere
   - These associations, while historically grounded, reflect Victorian-era gender ideology

3. **Absence of children's evidence**:
   - No toys or children's items documented
   - Could indicate:
     - Children weren't present
     - Children's items don't preserve well
     - Children's items not recognized/recorded
     - Children used adult items (common in working-class contexts)

4. **Limited evidence vs broad claims**:
   - Perfume bottles at 2 features provide evidence for women's presence at those specific locations
   - Broader claims about "family life" at the sites rely more on:
     - Nature of domestic assemblages
     - Comparison with known bachelor camp patterns
     - Historical context (some mining families documented in region)

---

## Recommendations for Funder Report

### What Can Be Confidently Stated:

✓ "Evidence of women's presence documented at 2 features through perfume bottles, a distinctly gendered personal care item"

✓ "Perfume bottles found in domestic contexts (hearth, waste pit) alongside household ceramics suggest women's participation in domestic life at the sites"

✓ "The presence of high-quality domestic assemblages with decorated ceramics and diverse tableware suggests household settings rather than transient bachelor camps"

### What Should Be Qualified:

⚠ "Evidence of family life" - Should be presented as:
- "Material culture consistent with household/family settings"
- Based on domestic assemblage patterns, not direct evidence
- Comparison with known bachelor camp assemblages

⚠ "Children's presence" - Should be stated as:
- "No direct archaeological evidence of children documented"
- "Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence"
- Historical records may provide complementary evidence

### Suggested Phrasing:

> "Archaeological evidence indicates women's presence at the mining sites, documented through perfume bottles—distinctly gendered personal care items—found in domestic contexts at two features. The broader material culture assemblages, including extensive domestic ceramics, quality tableware, and household items, suggest structured household settings rather than the transient bachelor camps often associated with mining operations. While direct archaeological evidence of children (such as toys) was not documented, the domestic assemblages and settlement patterns are consistent with family occupation."

## Conclusion

The archaeological evidence provides:

1. **Direct evidence**: Women's presence at 2 specific locations (perfume bottles)

2. **Indirect evidence**: Household/domestic patterns consistent with family occupation rather than bachelor camps (domestic assemblages, substantial buildings, quality ceramics)

3. **Absence**: No direct archaeological evidence of children

For the funder report, emphasize the **documented perfume bottles as concrete evidence** while presenting the family interpretation as a **plausible contextual interpretation** based on the broader material culture patterns, rather than direct archaeological evidence.
